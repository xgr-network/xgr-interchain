2. Self-custodial use and authorization

Users maintain sole control of their wallets, private keys and signing decisions. The XITA interface does not hold a user's private keys, sign a user's wallet transaction or make source lock/burn, onward bridge or destination delivery transactions on the user's behalf. All corresponding transactions require an external user-controlled wallet signature and payment of applicable native network gas. Optional sponsorship is a separate native-coin transfer to the wallet; it is not a mandate to execute a customer transaction.

Original ERC-20 tokens or native assets may nevertheless be locked in deployed public smart contracts. Such protocol escrows are not discretionary custody in a company wallet, but their code and security properties affect the availability of assets. Synthetic or wrapped tokens are distinct token contracts and are subject to on-chain proof and redemption mechanics.

3. Independent public protocol

Users may interact with the public ILN Gateways and Mailboxes directly, without using the commercial XITA Execution Gateway or an XITA-operated relayer. They must independently verify chain IDs, live contract addresses, route configuration, validator proof and delivery status, and fund every relevant chain's native gas and protocol fees. See the independent transfer documentation. Direct transactions may not be represented in the same XITA commercial journey UI.

4. Prices, fees and gas sponsorship

Depending on the chosen interface path, charges may include publicly defined on-chain protocol/validator fees, an optional separately quoted XITA commercial service fee, third-party network gas and ERC-20 allowance gas. Estimates are not a guarantee of final external network gas expenditure. The wallet displays blockchain transaction details before signing; an applicable commercial fee is disclosed by the interface before the customer authorizes its source transaction.

Gas sponsorship is voluntary, limited, stage-specific and conditional on an explicit availability indication, independently verified prior events, on-chain balances, risk limits and company reserves. Availability is not guaranteed, and support can be unavailable or exhausted. Users remain responsible for maintaining enough native gas on every chain unless a specific grant actually arrives in their own wallet. Sponsoring native gas is not a reimbursement or guarantee of settlement, and does not confer access to user assets or signing authority.

5. Transfer finality and delays

Cross-chain execution involves separate source and destination blockchain transactions, independently verified source message proofs, validator quorum and destination Mailbox processing. For external-chain to external-chain journeys, XGRChain is an intermediate hub requiring a separately authorized second hop. A source transaction can succeed while subsequent delivery is delayed or pending. Users should check the original message ID and on-chain destination status before attempting recovery. Repeating a source transaction may transfer additional assets and incur additional fees.
